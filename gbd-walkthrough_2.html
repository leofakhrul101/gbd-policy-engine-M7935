Governance-by-Design Policy Engine

This engine does not listen to music. It takes what a distributor already knows about a submission, applies rules that each trace back to an article of EU law, and produces one sealed record of how the decision was reached. Change the inputs below and watch the record change.

POLICY v0.1.0 IN FORCE FROM 2026-07-06 RULES 9 ARTICLES COVERED 8

Not legal advice. This is a readiness signal, not a finding that anyone has complied with anything. All data is made up. No real artists, labels or recordings.

Try a submission

Start from one of the four worked examples, or set the inputs yourself. Every figure shown is real output from the engine. There are 144 possible combinations and all of them were run through the code before this page was built.

Where a rule comes from

Each rule states which article of law it comes from, what that article requires, what the system must therefore do, the rule itself, and what ends up in the record. Pick a rule to read that chain.

LAW → WHAT IT REQUIRES → WHAT THE SYSTEM MUST DO → THE RULE → WHAT GOES IN THE RECORD

The point of holding this in the policy file rather than in a separate document is that a rule cannot be changed without its legal basis travelling with it.

Has the record been altered

Each record carries a hash of its own contents. Recomputing that hash tells you whether anything has been changed since the record was written. If one character moved, the check fails.

This proves the record has not been altered. It does not prove who wrote it. That would need a signing key, which this build does not have, and which the legal argument does not depend on.

Change the rules later

A threshold is moved and the policy becomes version 0.2.0. The same submission is now scored differently. But records already issued still name version 0.1.0, and they still produce the same result when run again under it.

This is what lets a decision made two years ago still be defended. It is judged against the rules that were actually in force at the time, not against whatever the policy says today.

What the artist is told

Every record carries a written explanation for the person affected. This is the part that has to meet the DSA standard: clear, easy to understand, and as precise as reasonably possible in the circumstances.

Whether this is good enough is not something the engine can judge about itself. It marks a statement as specific, thin or informational by counting how many facts it relied on, which is a structural stand-in for a question about quality. That gap is the argument the dissertation makes.

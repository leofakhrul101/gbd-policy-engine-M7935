The sealed record

Fourteen fields, hashed with SHA-256 over canonical JSON. Every triggered rule carries its norm references inside the hash, so the rule-to-norm link cannot drift from the decision it justified. Edit the record and the seal fails.

The Commission export

      
The falsifiable test, executed: every field the DSA Transparency Database requires, populated
         from the sealed record alone. Two requirements of Article 17 have nowhere to land — a finding
         about the schema, not about the artefact.

The field that is usually empty.
      decision_ground_reference is null in 99.9% of the 131 million statements Kaushal
      and colleagues analysed. Here it cannot be: the norm references travel inside the hashed
      payload, so the export projects them rather than asking anyone to supply them afterwards.

  






  
What this page is. A faithful browser port of gbd-policy-engine v0.2.1 —
     the same nine rules from policies/v0.2.0.yaml, the same evaluation order, the same
     canonical-JSON hashing. It was checked field-for-field and hash-for-hash against the Python
     implementation across 576 signal combinations; all 576 matched, including the SHA-256 digests
     and the UUIDv5 pack identifiers shown above. Nothing here is a mock-up of the engine's output.

  
What it is not. Synthetic signals, uncalibrated thresholds, and a single-operator log.
     The artefact records how a decision was reached; it does not make the decision correct.
